# Course-design research → checklist rule mapping

Companion reference for the [Course Checklist](README.md) plans. It records **where the checklist items come
from**, so that every rule the product shows an instructor can be traced to an established course-quality
standard rather than to an opinion. Rule packs [CC.3](CC.3-rule-pack-foundations-and-orientation.md),
[CC.4](CC.4-rule-pack-structure-outcomes-alignment.md),
[CC.5](CC.5-rule-pack-assessment-feedback-interaction.md) and
[CC.6](CC.6-rule-pack-accessibility-and-launch-readiness.md) cite this document; the citation chips rendered
next to each checklist item link here.

## 1. The sources

| Source | What it is | How we use it |
|---|---|---|
| **Quality Matters (QM) Higher Ed Rubric**, 7th ed. | 8 General Standards, 44 Specific Review Standards; the de-facto certification rubric for online/blended HE courses. Essential (3-point) standards must be met for certification. | Primary source for orientation, objectives, alignment, materials, interaction, technology, learner support and accessibility rules. |
| **SUNY OSCQR** (Online Course Quality Review Rubric), v4 | 50 standards in 6 categories, openly licensed, designed for formative course improvement rather than pass/fail certification. | Primary source for the concrete, checkable items — syllabus contents, layout, link text, tables, grading policies, gradebook, feedback. |
| **National Standards for Quality (NSQ) Online Courses** (formerly iNACOL) | 7 standards (A–G) aimed at K-12 online and blended programmes; crosswalked with the QM K-12 rubric. | K-12 framing, especially content standards alignment and course evaluation. |
| **CAST UDL Guidelines 3.0** | Three principles — Engagement, Representation, Action & Expression — plus the "who" of learning. | Source for the variety / multiple-means rules, and for framing accessibility as design rather than remediation. |
| **WCAG 2.1 AA** | Web content accessibility success criteria. | Legal floor for the CC.6 accessibility rules; cited by criterion number. |
| **Practitioner course-readiness checklists** (institutional pre-flight lists) | Operational lists used by universities before a term opens: publish the course, check due dates, verify links, confirm gradebook. | Source for the go-live rules that no design rubric covers. |

**What this is not.** Passing the checklist is not QM certification, not an OSCQR review, and not WCAG
conformance. The checklist automates the subset of these standards that is machine-checkable from course
data; the rest still needs human review. Every accessibility item states this in its help copy
([CC.6 FR-23](CC.6-rule-pack-accessibility-and-launch-readiness.md)).

## 2. Structure of the source rubrics

### Quality Matters — 8 General Standards

1. Course Overview and Introduction — the design is explained to the learner at the start.
2. Learning Objectives (Competencies) — what learners will be able to do.
3. Assessment and Measurement — assessments show how well objectives are met.
4. Instructional Materials — materials support the objectives.
5. Course Activities and Learner Interaction — activities support participation and engagement.
6. Course Technology — technology supports the objectives.
7. Learner Support — the course connects learners to support services.
8. Accessibility and Usability — a clear effort toward accessible, usable design.

### OSCQR — 6 categories, 50 standards

| Category | Standards | Theme |
|---|---|---|
| Overview and Information | 1–10 | Getting started, course overview, syllabus, printable syllabus, policies, learner-success resources, modality, access/participation, objectives, contact information |
| Technology and Tools | 11–15 | Requisite technology skills, scaffolding of technical skills, access to technology, privacy policies, accessible technology |
| Design and Layout | 16–28 | Navigation, white space, contrast, instructions, grammar/spelling, text formatting, blinking text, font and size, tables for layout, table accessibility, header rows/columns, slide layout/titles, slide transitions |
| Content and Activities | 29–37 | Variety, higher-order thinking, authentic activities, OER, copyright, text accessibility, text equivalents, graphics/images, hyperlink text |
| Interaction | 38–43 | Instructor feedback, interaction, instructor presence, class community, learner-to-learner interaction, learner contributions |
| Assessment and Feedback | 44–50 | Grading policies, learner assessment, assignment grading criteria, formative assessment, assessment accommodations, gradebook, course feedback |

### NSQ Online Courses — 7 standards

A Course Overview and Support · B Content · C Instructional Design · D Learner Assessment ·
E Accessibility and Usability · F Course Technology · G Course Evaluation.

### CAST UDL 3.0 — 3 principles

Engagement (the "why") · Representation (the "what") · Action & Expression (the "how"), with learner
identity woven across all three.

## 5. Design principles the catalog follows

1. **Every rule cites a source.** A proposed rule with no rubric standard and no operational justification is
   rejected in review ([CC.1 FR-2](../../completed/checklist/CC.1-checklist-registry-and-evaluation-engine.md)).
2. **Silence beats a false nag.** A rule whose feature is disabled, or whose course context makes it
   irrelevant, reports `not_applicable` and disappears — it does not count against progress.
3. **Progress, not failure.** Items report `13 / 24` rather than "incomplete", and completed items stay
   visible and crossed off.
4. **The instructor is the authority.** Every item can be dismissed with a reason; dismissal rates are how we
   find out a rule is wrong ([CC.10](CC.10-analytics-guidance-and-rollout.md)).
5. **No AI in the judgment.** All evaluators are deterministic heuristics so the checklist is free, offline-
   safe, privacy-neutral and reproducible. AI appears only in optional, human-approved *fixes*.
6. **No learner data where it is avoidable.** The whole of Pack B is FERPA-free by construction; the two
   accommodation rules in Pack C report counts and types only, never a student.
7. **Automated ≠ conformant.** The accessibility items say plainly that they are a partial automated check,
   not a WCAG conformance claim.
