# Community participation guide

Lextures participates to answer useful questions, not to manufacture endorsements. Before posting,
read `docs/plan/seo/offsite/authenticity-policy.md`, register the account, and review the current rules
of the specific community.

## Where and how

Prioritize communities where educators already ask questions: relevant education subreddits, the
Higher Ed Learning Collective, ISTE and state edtech groups, LinkedIn groups, and Hacker News for
open-source or self-hosting discussions. Presence on this list does not override local rules.

Every company-related contribution starts its disclosure in the body: “I work at Lextures — …”. Give
a complete, useful answer before mentioning the product. Link only when it is the strongest source.
If promotion is prohibited, contribute without promotion or do not participate.

## Cadence and response standard

The program owner assigns five substantive answers per week across a named-person rotation and checks
brand alerts daily. Respond to direct questions, including criticism, within 24 hours: acknowledge the
specific concern, state verifiable facts, disclose affiliation, offer a practical next step, and avoid
arguing. Record company-related mentions and responses in `mentions.md`.

Quarterly, audit 20 contributions for body disclosure, rule compliance, useful standalone content,
and absence of undisclosed promotion. Stop a channel if repeated participation is unwelcome.
